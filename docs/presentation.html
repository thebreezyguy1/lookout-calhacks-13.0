Devpost pitch (2–3 sentences)

Scams against older adults work because the scammer keeps them on the phone, rushed and sworn to secrecy, and existing tools only alert the family after the money is gone. Lookout watches a parent's accounts and credit file with their consent, and when it sees a scam pattern it opens a calm conversation on their screen that asks the questions that break the spell: are they still on the phone, were they told to keep it secret, did they ask for gift cards. Her answers become evidence that escalates to the family in real time, and a person approves any hold. Lookout never moves money.

Saturday afternoon, three stores, $1,500 in gift cards

A caller says he's her grandson and he's in jail. Each purchase on its own looks like ordinary shopping. Together they are the most common scam payment pattern there is. Mom is Dolores, a real CRS sandbox persona: 74, retired, every account closed for years.

What's new

The check-in is a conversation, and her answers are the strongest evidence

Watching accounts and alerting family already exists (EverSafe, Carefull, bank trusted-contact programs), and phone makers now flag scam calls and texts. Nobody connects the two: the bank sees a $500 gift-card purchase, the phone sees a "don't tell your parents" call, and neither knows they happened 20 minutes apart. Lookout links the conversation to the money, then steps in. A transaction rule can only guess. "He said not to tell my daughter" is close to proof.

Breaks the isolation

One short question at a time: are you on the phone with them right now (it's fine to hang up), did they tell you to keep it secret, did they ask for gift cards or the numbers on the back, did they say a loved one is in trouble.

Escalates live

Each answer becomes a weighted signal and goes back through the same action policy. In the demo, one $600 prepaid card goes from "Family alerted" (81%) to "Hold recommended" (99%) as she answers. The family watches the transcript as it happens.

Can't be talked down

Her answers can raise the risk but never lower it, because scammers coach victims to say "it's for me". Reassurance is recorded for the family instead. Claude replies that look like they ask for a code or password are thrown out.

How it works

Rules you can audit, Claude for judgment, a policy in charge

DataNessie bank activity (read-only), CRS credit reports from 3 bureaus plus Fraud Finder, and a labeled scenario injector for the demo.
Rules16 transaction rules and 10 identity signals, combined with a noisy-OR so weak signals add up and one strong signal is enough.
ClaudeLeads the conversation with Mom, names the pattern and writes the family summary. Every turn is structured output validated with Pydantic, with a scripted interview as fallback.
Policy + auditPicks the tier. Every decision is logged with the events, rule hits, reasoning, confidence and tier.

Claude can raise the risk but can only lower the rules' score by 0.15 at most, so a strong signal can't be argued away. If Claude fails or returns invalid output, the rules result stands.

What Claude changed, and what it didn't

  • Fixed Both rules-only false alarms (a car loan on an active file, a $48,250 closing wire to a known title company) drop to a gentle check-in.
  • Still short Gift cards labeled only "Apple Store" rise from no action to a check-in, below the expected alert.
  • New miss Two $3,000 cash withdrawals with no memo: Claude lowered it from alert to check-in. Its discount is capped at 0.15, which is why it stopped there.
  • Half of normal scenarios get a check-in. That's the trade: fewer alarms to the family, more quick questions to Mom.

Why the check-ins matter now

Every remaining miss lands on a check-in, and a check-in now opens the conversation. That's where Mom says "the bank's fraud department told me to" or "he said not to tell my daughter", and the tier climbs on her words. Scoring the conversation itself is the next eval to build.

The identity check doesn't treat credit quality as fraud: bankruptcy, collections and a low score stay clear, because that's the person's own history.

Setup

Laptop with the demo stage (/app/phone.html: scammer console, Mom's phone, what Lookout sees) and the family dashboard in a second tab. Hand the scammer console to the judge, or let them type their own scam.

With an Anthropic key set, Claude leads the conversation. Without one, or if the Wi-Fi drops, the scripted interview runs the same flow with no network.

If the Wi-Fi drops, run with use_llm=false; the scam flow needs no network. Only the CRS pulls do.

Responsible design

Built for her dignity as much as her money

Consent and visibility

Mom opts in, and her screen shows her the exact activity Lookout is asking about. It never shames, and never asks for passwords, codes or account numbers.

Read-only, always

No code path creates a transfer, purchase or withdrawal for her. Holds and freezes are recommendations a person approves.

Explainable

Each decision carries the events, the rule hits with weights, Claude's reasoning, confidence and tier, in an audit log the family can read.

Few false alarms

Low confidence caps the response at a check-in. Normal life (gifts, travel, the HOA) is part of the eval set on purpose.
